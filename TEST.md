Run this with one teacher (anyone — a colleague, a family member who teaches, anyone). Numbers go straight into the pitch as the "10× faster decision" claim.

## The test (3 minutes)

Sit the teacher down. Read the prompt:

> *"Sul on 8.A klassi murdude põhitehete tulemused. Otsusta kahte asja: (1) kes vajab tuge, (2) kas teha kordus või liikuda järgmise teema juurde. Vajuta stoppi kui oled valmis."*

**Round 1 — paper:**
- Start a stopwatch
- Hand them the printed table
- Stop when they tell you their decision
- Note: time, decision, confidence on 1–10

**Round 2 — EduNavi:**
- Reset the stopwatch
- Open `https://edunavi.vercel.app/class` on a laptop
- Stop when they tell you their decision
- Note: time, decision, confidence on 1–10

## What you'll likely see

Per the strategy doc's hypothesis: paper takes 5–10 minutes for an unfamiliar teacher, EduNavi takes 10–30 seconds. Even if your test gets 3 min vs 30 sec, you have a real number — *"10× kiirem otsus"* — backed by an A/B test you actually ran.

## How to use it in the pitch

Open with: *"Me testisime ühe õpetajaga eile. Paberil 4 minutit, EduNaviga 25 sekundit. Sama otsus, sama kindlustunne."*

Even one data point beats a hypothesis.

## If the test misses or runs late

Skip the live numbers. The recommendation engine still produces a believable demo. But if you have *any* number, use it.
